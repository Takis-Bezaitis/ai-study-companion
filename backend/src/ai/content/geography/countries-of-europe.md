# 1. Introduction to Europe

## 1.1 What is Europe?

Europe is one of the world's traditionally recognized continents. It occupies the western part of the Eurasian landmass and is bounded by the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Arctic Ocean to the north. To the south it is separated from Africa mainly by the Mediterranean Sea, while its conventional eastern boundary with Asia is commonly drawn through areas including the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, the Greater Caucasus, and the Black Sea region.

Europe is relatively small compared with Asia, Africa, and the Americas, but it contains a large variety of landscapes. These include Arctic tundra, broad plains, forests, high mountain ranges, Mediterranean coasts, large river basins, islands, and densely populated lowlands.

Europe's geography has strongly influenced settlement, agriculture, transportation, trade, and the development of cities. Rivers provided transportation routes and water resources, mountain ranges created natural barriers, and seas connected coastal communities.

## 1.2 Europe as a Geographical Concept

The exact definition of Europe is not based on a single universally agreed physical boundary. Europe and Asia form one continuous landmass, Eurasia, so the boundary between them is partly conventional.

Most of Europe is clearly situated west of the conventional Europe–Asia boundary. Several countries, however, have territories that lie on both sides of commonly used geographic boundaries, or are associated with Europe for historical, cultural, or political-geographic reasons.

For this lesson:

* The main country section covers the countries conventionally treated as European.
* Transcontinental or geographically debated cases are identified explicitly.
* Kosovo is included in a separate profile because it is commonly included in educational lists of European countries, while its international status is not recognized uniformly.
* Membership in the European Union is **not** used as the definition of being a European country. Europe contains many countries that are not EU members.

## 1.3 Size and Geographic Diversity

Europe contains countries ranging from very large territories to microstates.

Examples include:

* **Russia** is by far the largest country associated with Europe geographically, although most of its territory lies in Asia.
* **Ukraine** is the largest country whose entire territory lies within the commonly recognized geographic area of Europe.
* **France, Spain, Sweden, Norway, Germany, Poland, and Finland** are among Europe's larger countries.
* **Vatican City, Monaco, San Marino, Liechtenstein, Andorra, and Malta** are among the smallest European states.

Europe also contains major islands and island groups, including Iceland, Ireland, Great Britain, Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, Crete, Cyprus, and the Balearic Islands.

# 2. European Regions

## 2.1 Why Divide Europe into Regions?

Geographers use regions to make a large and diverse area easier to study. Regional boundaries are not always identical between different geographic systems.

A useful educational division is:

* Northern Europe
* Western Europe
* Central Europe
* Eastern Europe
* Southern Europe
* Southeastern Europe
* The Balkans
* The Nordic region
* The Baltic region

These categories can overlap. For example, a country can be considered both Northern European and Nordic, or both Southern European and Balkan.

## 2.2 Northern Europe

Northern Europe generally includes countries around the northern Atlantic, North Sea, Baltic Sea, and Scandinavian Peninsula.

Countries commonly associated with Northern Europe include:

* Denmark
* Estonia
* Finland
* Iceland
* Ireland
* Latvia
* Lithuania
* Norway
* Sweden
* United Kingdom

The region contains fjords, rocky coasts, forests, lakes, islands, and northern climates. Parts of Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Iceland extend into areas with very long winter nights and long summer daylight.

## 2.3 The Nordic Region

The Nordic countries are:

* Denmark
* Finland
* Iceland
* Norway
* Sweden

The Nordic region is a cultural and geographical grouping rather than a strict physical region.

The Scandinavian Peninsula is mainly occupied by Norway and Sweden. Denmark is Scandinavian in the cultural and historical sense but is located on the Jutland Peninsula and nearby islands rather than on the Scandinavian Peninsula.

Finland and Iceland are Nordic but are not normally classified as Scandinavian in the strict geographic sense.

## 2.4 The Baltic Region

The three Baltic states are:

* Estonia
* Latvia
* Lithuania

They are located on the eastern shore of the Baltic Sea.

The Baltic Sea also borders or is closely connected to:

* Sweden
* Finland
* Russia
* Poland
* Germany
* Denmark

The Baltic region is characterized by low-lying coastal landscapes, forests, islands, and numerous bays and straits.

## 2.5 Western Europe

Western Europe commonly includes:

* Belgium
* France
* Ireland
* Luxembourg
* Netherlands
* United Kingdom

Switzerland and Austria are sometimes included in Western Europe in broad classifications, although they are also strongly associated with Central Europe.

Western Europe contains the Atlantic coast, the English Channel, the North Sea, major river systems, fertile lowlands, and important mountain areas such as the Alps and Pyrenees.

## 2.6 Central Europe

Central Europe is a geographic and cultural region rather than a single universally defined group.

Countries commonly associated with Central Europe include:

* Austria
* Czechia
* Germany
* Hungary
* Liechtenstein
* Poland
* Slovakia
* Slovenia
* Switzerland

Parts of Croatia and other neighboring countries can also be described as Central European depending on the classification being used.

The region contains the Alps, the Carpathians, the Danube Basin, the Central European Plain, and numerous important river valleys.

## 2.7 Eastern Europe

Eastern Europe commonly includes:

* Belarus
* Moldova
* Russia
* Ukraine

Some broader classifications also include countries farther south or east.

Eastern Europe contains extensive plains, forests, large rivers, wetlands, and continental climates. The East European Plain is one of the major physical regions of the continent.

## 2.8 Southern Europe

Southern Europe generally includes countries around the Mediterranean Sea and the southern European peninsulas.

These include:

* Greece
* Italy
* Malta
* Portugal
* Spain

Croatia, Slovenia, Albania, Montenegro, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and other Balkan countries are also often associated with Southern Europe or Southeastern Europe.

Southern Europe has many Mediterranean landscapes, mountainous areas, islands, peninsulas, and long coastlines.

## 2.9 Southeastern Europe and the Balkans

Southeastern Europe includes the Balkan Peninsula and surrounding areas.

Countries commonly associated with the Balkans include:

* Albania
* Bosnia and Herzegovina
* Bulgaria
* Croatia
* Greece
* Kosovo
* Montenegro
* North Macedonia
* Serbia
* Slovenia

Romania and Turkey are sometimes included in broader definitions.

The Balkans are characterized by rugged mountains, river valleys, karst landscapes, inland basins, and complex coastlines, especially along the Adriatic, Ionian, Aegean, and Black Seas.

# 5. Major European Geographic Features

## 5.1 The European Plain

The European Plain is one of the continent's most important lowland regions.

It extends across large parts of northern and eastern Europe and includes extensive areas of:

* agricultural land
* grassland
* forests
* river basins
* densely populated lowlands

The plain is particularly important in countries such as France, Belgium, Netherlands, Germany, Poland, Belarus, Ukraine, and Russia.

## 5.2 The Iberian Peninsula

The Iberian Peninsula contains:

* Spain
* Portugal
* Andorra
* a small British territory at its southern tip

Major physical features include the Meseta Central, Pyrenees, Sierra Nevada, Ebro Basin, and several major Atlantic and Mediterranean river systems.

## 5.3 The Italian Peninsula

The Italian Peninsula extends southward into the Mediterranean.

Its principal mountain system is the Apennines, while the Alps form the northern mountain barrier.

The Po Valley lies between the Alps and northern Apennines and is one of Italy's most important lowland regions.

## 5.4 The Balkan Peninsula

The Balkan Peninsula is a mountainous region in southeastern Europe.

Important mountain systems include:

* Dinaric Alps
* Balkan Mountains
* Pindus Mountains
* Šar Mountains
* Rhodope Mountains

The region contains several important seas and gulfs, including the Adriatic, Ionian, Aegean, and Black Seas.

## 5.5 The Scandinavian Peninsula

The Scandinavian Peninsula is primarily occupied by:

* Norway
* Sweden

Its western side contains rugged mountains and deeply indented fjords, while eastern and southern areas contain broader lowlands, forests, lakes, and coastal plains.

# 11. Important Geographic Superlatives

## 11.1 Largest Country Associated with Europe

Russia is the largest country associated geographically with Europe because its European territory is part of the continent, even though most of Russia's total land area lies in Asia.

## 11.2 Largest Country Entirely in Europe

Ukraine is commonly described as the largest country whose entire national territory lies within Europe.

## 11.3 Longest River in Europe

The Volga is Europe's longest river.

It flows entirely through Russia and empties into the Caspian Sea.

## 11.4 Major River Crossing Many Countries

The Danube is particularly notable for passing through or along many European countries and for connecting Central Europe with the Black Sea.

## 11.5 Highest Mountain in Europe

The answer depends on the geographic definition of the Europe–Asia boundary.

If the Caucasus is considered part of Europe, Mount Elbrus in Russia is generally regarded as Europe's highest mountain.

If a different boundary is used, Mont Blanc in the Alps is often cited as the highest mountain of Western or conventional continental Europe.

For educational questions, the boundary convention should therefore be specified when asking for Europe's highest mountain.

## 11.6 Europe's Smallest States

The European microstates include:

* Vatican City
* Monaco
* San Marino
* Liechtenstein
* Andorra
* Malta

They differ greatly in physical geography. For example, Liechtenstein and Andorra are mountainous, while Malta and Monaco have Mediterranean coastal environments.

# 12. Interesting but Factual Geography Facts

## 12.1 Europe Is Part of Eurasia

Europe is not separated from Asia by a complete ocean. Instead, the two form the connected landmass of Eurasia.

The Europe–Asia boundary is a conventional geographic boundary.

## 12.2 The Danube Connects Central Europe to the Black Sea

The Danube begins in Germany and travels across Central and Southeastern Europe before reaching the Black Sea.

This makes its drainage basin one of Europe's major geographic networks.

## 12.3 The Netherlands Contains Extensive Low-Lying Land

Large areas of the Netherlands are at or below sea level. Dikes, dunes, drainage systems, canals, pumping stations, and other water-management infrastructure help protect and manage these areas.

## 12.4 Norway's Fjords Were Shaped by Glaciers

Many Norwegian fjords were formed when glaciers carved deep valleys into the landscape. After the glaciers retreated, seawater flooded the valleys.

## 12.5 Iceland Lies on a Major Plate Boundary

Iceland is located along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates move apart.

This helps explain Iceland's volcanic and geothermal activity.

## 12.6 The Alps Are a Major European Watershed

The Alps influence the drainage systems of several major European rivers.

Important rivers associated with Alpine sources or regions include:

* Rhine
* Rhône
* Po
* Inn
* tributaries of the Danube

## 12.7 The Baltic Sea Is Relatively Enclosed

The Baltic Sea has a limited connection to the North Sea through Danish straits.

Its restricted connection with the Atlantic influences its salinity and water circulation.

## 12.8 The Mediterranean Has Many Subregions

The Mediterranean Sea is not geographically uniform. It includes several recognizable seas and subregions, including:

* Adriatic Sea
* Aegean Sea
* Ionian Sea
* Tyrrhenian Sea
* Ligurian Sea
* Alboran Sea

## 12.9 Europe Has Many Peninsulas

Major European peninsulas include:

* Scandinavian Peninsula
* Iberian Peninsula
* Italian Peninsula
* Balkan Peninsula
* Jutland Peninsula

Peninsulas give Europe a very long and complex coastline relative to its land area.

## 12.10 Europe Has Many Important Islands

Examples include:

* Great Britain
* Ireland
* Iceland
* Sicily
* Sardinia
* Corsica
* Crete
* Cyprus
* Malta
* Balearic Islands

Islands contribute significantly to Europe's geographic diversity.

## 13.2 Island

An island is land completely surrounded by water.

Examples in Europe include:

* Iceland
* Ireland
* Sicily
* Sardinia
* Crete
* Cyprus

## 13.3 Archipelago

An archipelago is a group or chain of islands.

Examples associated with Europe include the Greek islands and the islands of the Stockholm archipelago.

## 13.4 Fjord

A fjord is a long, narrow, deep inlet created when a glacial valley is flooded by the sea.

Norway is particularly famous for fjords.

## 13.5 Delta

A river delta forms where a river deposits sediment as it enters a slower-moving body of water.

The Danube Delta on the Black Sea is one of Europe's best-known river deltas.

## 13.6 Basin

A drainage basin is an area of land from which water drains toward a common river system or outlet.

Major European basins include:

* Danube Basin
* Rhine Basin
* Volga Basin
* Dnieper Basin

## 13.7 Watershed

A watershed or drainage divide separates areas whose water flows into different river systems.

Mountain ranges often form important watersheds.

## 13.8 Strait

A strait is a narrow body of water connecting two larger bodies of water.

Important European examples include:

* Strait of Gibraltar
* English Channel
* Bosporus
* Dardanelles
* Øresund

# 14. Geographic Connections Between Countries

## 14.1 The Rhine Network

The Rhine connects Alpine Europe with western European lowlands.

Its drainage system is associated with:

* Switzerland
* Liechtenstein
* Austria
* Germany
* France
* Netherlands

It eventually reaches the North Sea.

## 14.2 The Danube Network

The Danube creates a geographic connection from Central Europe toward the Black Sea.

Its basin connects numerous countries and major cities.

## 14.3 The Alpine Region

The Alps cross several national boundaries and create shared geographic systems involving:

* Switzerland
* France
* Italy
* Austria
* Germany
* Slovenia
* Liechtenstein
* Monaco

The mountains influence climate, river drainage, tourism, agriculture, transportation, and settlement.

## 14.4 The Balkan Mountain System

The Balkans contain several connected mountain systems and river valleys.

Geographic features such as the Dinaric Alps, Balkan Mountains, Pindus Mountains, and Šar Mountains contribute to the region's complex terrain.

## 14.5 The Iberian River Systems

The Iberian Peninsula contains major rivers including:

* Tagus
* Ebro
* Douro
* Guadiana
* Guadalquivir

Several of these rivers cross national boundaries between Spain and Portugal.

## 15.7 Essential Facts for Review

* Europe and Asia form the connected landmass of **Eurasia**.
* The **Alps** are one of Europe's major mountain systems.
* The **Volga** is Europe's longest river.
* The **Danube** is one of Europe's most internationally important rivers.
* The **Rhine** flows from the Alps toward the North Sea.
* The **Pyrenees** separate the Iberian Peninsula from France.
* The **Apennines** run through Italy.
* The **Scandinavian Mountains** extend mainly through Norway and Sweden.
* The **Dinaric Alps** are a major mountain system of the western Balkans.
* The **Baltic Sea** lies in northern Europe.
* The **Mediterranean Sea** lies south of Europe.
* The **Black Sea** lies between southeastern Europe and Western Asia.
* The **Caspian Sea** lies between Europe and Asia.
* **Norway** is known for its fjords and mountainous coastline.
* **Iceland** lies on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and has extensive volcanic and geothermal features.
* The **Netherlands** contains extensive low-lying and reclaimed land.
* **Switzerland** is a landlocked Alpine country.
* **Greece** has a highly mountainous mainland and many islands.
* **Italy** includes the Alps, Apennines, Po Valley, Sicily, and Sardinia.
* **Spain and Portugal** occupy most of the Iberian Peninsula.
* **Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden** form the Nordic countries.
* **Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania** are the Baltic states.
* **Germany** has nine neighboring countries.
* **Austria** has eight neighboring countries.
* Europe contains several **microstates**, including Vatican City, Monaco, San Marino, Liechtenstein, Andorra, and Malta.
* Some countries near the Europe–Asia boundary, such as **Russia, Türkiye, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, and Kazakhstan**, require an explicit geographic convention when discussing whether they are wholly or partly European.
* **Cyprus** is geographically located in the eastern Mediterranean but is commonly included in European political and cultural geography.
* **Kosovo** is commonly included in geographic lists of European countries, while its international recognition as a state is not universal.
